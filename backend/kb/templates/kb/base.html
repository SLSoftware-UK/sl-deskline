{% comment %} Header auth block. Three states — and the line between the last two is the one to get right, because "staff" and "customer" are decided by Django's is_staff flag and nothing else: 1. Anonymous — a Log in link carrying ?next=, so a reader comes back to the article they were on. /login/ is the password form by default, or, with SSO enabled, a page that links out to the identity provider (accounts/views.py's module docstring explains both). 2. Customer — any signed-in account without is_staff. Their organisations (accounts.Membership, supplied by the user_organisations context processor) are shown next to their name because that is what their tickets are scoped to: someone in two organisations needs to see both before raising a ticket. A customer in no organisation is still a customer (they can raise organisation-less tickets) and just shows no names. 3. Support agent — an is_staff/is_superuser account. They work every organisation's tickets and belong to none of them, so no organisation is shown. A superuser also has the authoring sidebar and its own log-out control, so they see two. That is left alone on purpose: a non-superuser support agent gets no sidebar and still needs this one, and special-casing the overlap would make the header stop matching the three states above for the sake of one duplicate button. Log out is the same POST form in both signed-in states: accounts:logout is @require_POST (a GET logout is CSRF-able and pre-fetchable, and it flushes the session server-side, which matters on shared workstations), so it needs a real form, not a link. {% endcomment %} {% if not request.user.is_authenticated %}
